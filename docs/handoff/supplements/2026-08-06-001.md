## The two-window staging problem, and how it was handled

Twice today, Window 1 and Window 3 had genuinely unrelated, non-overlapping uncommitted
work live in `/opt/nemesis` at the same time. A plain `git commit` takes the whole index,
not just the paths one window intends — so every commit in both cases was staged by
exact path (`git add <path>` per file, never `-A`), then verified in isolation via
`git show --stat` before pushing, confirming nothing from the other window's WIP had been
swept in. This happened for `aa6916c`/`db9e0c4` and again around `272db73`. No
cross-contamination occurred in either case; the discipline held.

## Process notes worth carrying forward

- **The DHCP grant review is the clearest example this session of "verify against
  reality, not the label."** The report describing the delivery was detailed, tested, and
  written by someone who had just finished eliminating four instances of "checks matching
  my own docstring/comment rather than code" — and the one check meant to guard the fifth
  property (grant precision) was itself a sixth instance of exactly that class, hiding in
  plain sight. Read code claims skeptically even from a careful, self-aware author;
  especially then, since the report's own confidence made it easy to trust.
- **Window 2 cannot message another window's terminal session directly.** When a finding
  needed to go back to Window 1 for a fix rather than being deferred, the available
  channel was the public PUNCHLIST.md (the established cross-window "found a bug" venue)
  plus a prominent HANDOFF.md section — not a real-time signal. The operator relayed it.
- **The power-risk framing (mid-afternoon storm) changed HANDOFF.md's shape usefully**: a
  cold-start summary leading the file, kept current after every step rather than only at
  natural stopping points, made the several hours of held/resolved state (the DHCP grant
  especially) far easier to track than prose buried mid-file would have been. Worth
  reusing that structure for any future session with a real interruption risk, not just
  storms.
- **Live worklog discipline held today**, unlike 08-04/08-05's gap — written to
  continuously rather than reconstructed at closeout, specifically because the power-risk
  framing made "assume this ends here" the working assumption for several hours.
